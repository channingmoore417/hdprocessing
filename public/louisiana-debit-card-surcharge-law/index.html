New Louisiana Law · In Effect Since Aug 1, 2026

Louisiana's New Debit Card Law, Explained.

As of August 1, 2026, Louisiana businesses can no longer add a fee when a customer pays with a debit card. If your terminal charges a card fee, this probably affects you — and the fix is usually a simple settings change. Here's the law in plain English, plus a free way to make sure you're set.

The Short Version

What Changed On August 1

Act 751 — introduced as Senate Bill 254 and now codified at La. R.S. 51:3081 — does one specific thing: it ends the practice of adding an extra charge when a customer pays with a debit card. Everything else about how you price and take payments stays the same.

The Rule

No fees on debit payments

A customer paying with a debit card pays the posted price — no card fee, service fee, or "non-cash adjustment" added on top.

Who It Covers

Most Louisiana businesses

The law covers retail sellers broadly — restaurants, shops, salons, and service businesses. If you sell to the public and take debit cards, plan on it applying to you.

What Didn't Change

Everything else

Single pricing, true cash discounts, and credit-card surcharge programs (with debit properly excluded) all continue exactly as before.

Side by Side

What Works Now — And What Doesn't

✓ Still perfectly fine

  • One posted price for everyone, however they pay.
  • A true cash discount off a single posted price.
  • A credit-card surcharge — as long as your system reliably keeps it off debit.

– Ended by Act 751

  • A fee added at checkout for paying with a debit card.
  • A "credit-card fee" that also lands on debit because the terminal can't tell the two apart.
  • The same fee under a different label — "service fee," "convenience fee," "non-cash adjustment" — when it reaches debit.

Structure Matters

Two Ways to Offset Card Costs — One Still Works

Plenty of Louisiana businesses offset processing costs, and you still can. What matters now is how the math is structured on the receipt.

Cash discount

Works
Posted price (everyone)$51.75
− Cash discount (3.5%)− $1.75
Cash customer pays$50.00

Why it works: there's one posted price, and paying cash earns a discount off it. Nothing extra is ever added for using a debit card — so Act 751 never comes into play.

Card fee on debit

Ended
Item price$50.00
+ Card fee (3.5%)+ $1.75
Debit customer pays$51.75

Why it doesn't: this starts from a base price and adds an amount at checkout for paying by card. When that added amount can reach a debit card, it's the structure Act 751 ended.

The customer can end up paying the same either way — the difference is whether the cash price is a discount or the card price is an add-on. If you like your current program, it can usually be restructured the compliant way without changing your margins.

Worth Knowing

Why Well-Run Businesses End Up Out of Step

This usually isn't anyone being sneaky. Two quirks of how card payments work catch people out.

01

Debit doesn't always look like debit

A debit card can run as PIN debit, or route through a credit network as "signature debit" — just a tap or swipe, no PIN. It looks like credit at the counter, but legally it's still a debit card, however it's processed.

02

Terminals apply fees to everything

Many surcharge programs were set up by a processor years ago, configured to add the fee to every card. If nobody ever told the terminal to treat debit differently, it doesn't — and the owner rarely knows.

✓

If this might be you, it's very fixable.

In most cases it's a configuration change or a switch to a compliant cash-discount structure — often done the same day, with no change to your effective margins. That's the kind of adjustment we handle for Lafayette businesses all the time.

Good to Know

How the Law Handles Mistakes

Act 751 was written with a built-in path to make things right. Here's how it works, factually and without the drama.

The Cure Window

30 days to make it right

If a customer sends written notice, a business has 30 days to correct the issue and refund the surcharge. Doing so resolves that private claim — the law rewards fixing problems promptly.

Consumer Complaints

The AG takes reports

Customers can raise unresolved issues with the Louisiana Attorney General's Consumer Protection Hotline (1-800-351-4889) or its online form. Continued violations of an order can carry civil penalties of up to $500 each.

Private Claims

For uncured cases

Customers can pursue actual damages for violations that are willful, repeated, or left uncured. In practice: fix issues when you learn about them, and this provision stays theoretical.

The takeaway isn't alarm — it's that checking your setup once beats finding out from a customer letter. A five-minute look at a statement or receipt usually settles it.

Background

Where the Law Came From

The bill was authored by Senator Beth Mizell of District 12, who chairs the Senate Committee on Commerce, Consumer Protection, and International Affairs. It was positioned as a consumer-protection measure bringing Louisiana in line with the federal Dodd-Frank framework on debit transactions, and it drew support from both parties — passing the Senate without a single no vote before Governor Jeff Landry signed it on June 2, 2026.

i

Scope check: Act 751 is about debit cards only. It doesn't change the rules for credit-card surcharges or cash discounts.

In other words: this is settled law, not a proposal to watch. The practical question for a business owner is simply whether your card processing and POS setup reflect it — and that's a five-minute check.

Common Questions

Act 751 Questions, Answered Simply

Can a Louisiana business charge a fee for debit card payments?+
Not anymore. Act 751 (formerly Senate Bill 254) took effect August 1, 2026 and ended extra charges on debit card payments. Credit-card surcharges and true cash discounts aren't affected by this law.
Does the law apply to my business?+
If you sell to the public in Louisiana and accept debit cards, plan on yes — restaurants, retail, salons, and service businesses are all covered. The more useful question is whether your current card setup lines up with it, which the 60-second self-check above will tell you.
Are cash discounts still allowed?+
Yes. One posted price for everyone, with a discount off that price for cash, remains completely legal. What ended is the reverse: a base price with a fee added at checkout that can land on debit. Same math, different structure — see the side-by-side comparison above.
Can I still surcharge credit cards?+
Yes — Act 751 doesn't restrict credit-card surcharges. The practical requirement is that your system genuinely excludes debit. Because debit cards often route through credit networks with just a tap or swipe, it's worth verifying with a real debit-card test rather than assuming.
What if I've been charging debit fees without realizing it?+
You're not alone — most affected businesses had no idea, because a processor configured the program. The law gives you a path: after a customer's written notice, you have 30 days to fix the issue and refund the surcharge, which resolves that claim. Better still is fixing it before any notice arrives — usually a quick configuration change or a restructure to a compliant cash discount.
How do I check what my terminal actually does?+
Two ways: run a small purchase on a real debit card and compare the receipt to your posted price, and look at your merchant statement, which shows whether a surcharge program is active and how it's set up. If you'd rather not decode a statement yourself, we'll review it free.

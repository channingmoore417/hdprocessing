The Law in Plain English

What Act 751 Actually Does

Senate Bill 254 became Act 751 when Governor Jeff Landry signed it on June 2, 2026 — same law, new name. On the books it's codified at La. R.S. 51:3081. It bans one specific thing: adding an extra charge when a customer pays with a debit card. That one specific thing is where most of the risk hides.

Debit Only

It's about debit cards

Act 751 bans surcharging debit cards. Surcharging credit cards isn't prohibited by this law — but only if debit is reliably excluded.

Who It Covers

Nearly every business

The law reaches retail sellers broadly. If you sell to the public and accept debit cards, assume it applies to you — restaurants, retail, salons, services.

Since When

In effect since Aug 1, 2026

Signed into law and now in force. Curing a violation means reimbursing every surcharged customer within 30 days of written notice.

If You Get It Wrong

The Penalties Have Teeth

§
01 · Private Lawsuits

Customers can sue you

Act 751 creates a private right of action. A single customer can bring a claim for actual damages — no class action required — for willful, repeated, or uncured violations.

§
02 · State Enforcement

A hotline straight to the AG

Surcharges can be reported to the Louisiana Attorney General today via the Consumer Protection Hotline — 1-800-351-4889 — or the online complaint form. Defying an administrative or court order costs up to $500 per violation, plus the AG's legal costs.

§
03 · The Clock

A 30-day cure window

After a customer's written notice, you have 30 days to fix the violation and reimburse the surcharge to stop a private suit. It doesn't cover willful or repeated violations — and it doesn't stop the Attorney General.

Card Network Rules + Act 751
Double Exposure

"The card brands never flagged me" isn't the cover it used to be.

Surcharging debit already violated Visa and Mastercard rules — but enforcement was spotty, and many businesses never heard a word. Act 751 doesn't change what's prohibited; it changes who can act on it. The same surcharge now opens you up to customer lawsuits and state enforcement, not just network penalties.

✕ What's prohibited

  • Adding a fee or surcharge for paying by debit card.
  • A "credit-card surcharge" that also lands on debit because your system can't tell them apart.
  • A surcharge relabeled as a "service fee" or "non-cash adjustment" that still hits debit.

✓ What's still allowed

  • Charging one price for everyone, no matter how they pay.
  • A genuine cash discount off a single posted price.
  • Surcharging credit cards only — if debit is reliably and verifiably excluded.

The Line That Decides It

Surcharge vs. Cash Discount

They can cost the customer the exact same dollar — but only one of them is legal now. The difference is structure, not price.

Debit surcharge

Prohibited

+ Card fee (4%)+ $4.00

Why it's a surcharge: there's a base price, and an extra amount gets added at checkout for paying by card. The moment that fee can reach a debit card, it's exactly what the law bans.

Cash discount

Allowed
Posted price (all)$104.00
− Cash discount (4%)− $4.00

Why it's legal: one posted price for everyone, and cash buyers get a discount off it. No extra amount is ever added for using a debit card.

Same $4 gap. One adds a fee to the card price; the other discounts the cash price. Whether your program is built — and disclosed — the legal way is exactly what our free check looks at.

The Most Common Trap

"But I Just Swipe the Card — Aren't I Fine?"

No — and this is where many Louisiana businesses get caught.

01

Debit runs two ways

A debit card can process as PIN debit (customer enters a PIN) or route as signature debit through a credit network — often just a swipe or tap, no PIN. To your customer it feels the same. To the law, it's a debit card either way, and Act 751 bans surcharging it regardless of how it's run.

02

Your terminal may not know

Many point-of-sale systems either can't tell debit from credit or were never programmed to exclude debit from a card surcharge. A business that believes it's only surcharging credit can be surcharging debit on every swipe — without realizing it.

Your processor isn't liable for this. You are.

The law applies to the retail business, not the company that set up your equipment. If your terminal is surcharging debit, that's your exposure — even if you didn't configure it and didn't know.

About the Law

Settled Law, With Bipartisan Backing

SB 254 was authored by Senator Beth Mizell (R, District 12), chair of the Senate Committee on Commerce, Consumer Protection, and International Affairs. It was framed as a consumer-protection measure that aligns Louisiana with the existing federal prohibition on debit-card surcharges under the Dodd-Frank Act, and it was signed into law by Governor Jeff Landry after passing the Senate unanimously and the House overwhelmingly.

!

Important: Act 751 applies only to debit card transactions. It does not change the rules for credit-card surcharges.

The takeaway: this is settled law — signed by the Governor, supported across both parties, and in effect since August 1, 2026. The only question left is whether your payment setup complies. If it doesn't, fixing it usually starts with an honest look at your credit card processing and POS system configuration.

Questions, Answered

Act 751, In Plain English

Is it legal to charge a debit card fee in Louisiana?+
No — not since August 1, 2026. Act 751 (formerly SB 254) prohibits charging a customer an extra amount for paying with a debit card. Credit-card surcharging isn't banned by this law, and genuine cash discounts remain legal — the line the law draws is debit.
Does Act 751 apply to my business, including restaurants?+
Almost certainly, if you sell to the public in Louisiana and accept debit cards. Restaurants, retail shops, salons, and service businesses are all in scope — the law reaches retail sellers broadly. The real question isn't whether it applies, but whether the way you take cards complies. The risk scorecard walks you through it in four questions.
Is cash discounting still legal in Louisiana?+
Yes — a genuine cash discount is allowed. The key is structure: one posted price everyone sees, with cash buyers getting a discount off it. That's different from a base price with a fee added for paying by card, which is a surcharge in substance even if it's labeled a discount. See the surcharge vs. cash discount comparison above.
Can I still surcharge credit cards?+
Act 751 targets debit cards only — it doesn't prohibit credit-card surcharges. But that's only safe if debit is reliably and verifiably excluded. The common trap is a "credit-only" surcharge that the point-of-sale system can't actually keep off debit, so debit gets charged anyway.
What are the penalties?+
Two channels. First, a private right of action — a customer can sue for actual damages, no class action required, for willful, repeated, or uncured violations. Second, the Attorney General can act on complaints from the Consumer Protection Hotline (1-800-351-4889) or online complaint form, backed by the customer's own receipt; defying an administrative or court order carries civil penalties of up to $500 per violation. Curing — fixing the violation and reimbursing the surcharge within 30 days of written notice — stops a private suit, but not the Attorney General.
How do I know if my current setup is compliant?+
Most merchants who surcharge debit are doing it unknowingly, because a processor configured it. The fastest way to find out is your processing statement — it shows whether you're surcharging, at what rate, and through whom. Take the risk scorecard for an instant read, or request a free compliance check and we'll confirm it from your statement.

Note: surcharge complaints currently go through the Attorney General's general Consumer Protection Hotline (1-800-351-4889) and online complaint form; a dedicated Act 751 reporting channel hasn't been announced.
